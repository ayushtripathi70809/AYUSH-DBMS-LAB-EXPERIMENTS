# Experiment 1: ER Diagram for an Indian E-Commerce Platform

## Aim

To design an Entity-Relationship (ER) diagram for an Indian e-commerce platform using Customer, Product, Order, OrderItem, Seller, Category, Payment, Delivery, and Address entities.

## Description

This experiment focuses on designing an ER diagram for an Indian e-commerce platform. It represents entities, attributes, relationships, and constraints required to manage customers, products, orders, sellers, categories, payments, deliveries, and addresses.

## Entities

1. **Customer** – Stores customer details.
2. **Product** – Stores product information.
3. **Order** – Stores customer order details.
4. **OrderItem** – Represents individual products within an order.
5. **Seller** – Stores seller information.
6. **Category** – Classifies products into different categories.
7. **Payment** – Stores payment transaction details.
8. **Delivery** – Stores shipment and delivery information.
9. **Address** – Stores customer addresses.

## Concepts Used

* Entity-Relationship (ER) Model
* Primary Keys and Foreign Keys
* Composite Attributes
* Multivalued Attributes
* Weak Entities
* Product Specialization
* Relationships and Cardinality
* Total and Partial Participation Constraints

## Design Requirements

* Define primary keys for all entities.
* Represent composite attributes, such as CustomerName and Address.
* Identify multivalued attributes, such as customer phone numbers.
* Model OrderItem as a weak entity dependent on Order.
* Represent Product specialization, such as Electronics, Clothing, and Grocery.
* Specify relationship cardinalities and participation constraints.

## Expected Output

An ER diagram showing all nine entities, their attributes, primary keys, relationships, cardinalities, specialization, weak entities, and participation constraints.

## Result

The ER diagram for the Indian e-commerce platform is designed successfully, representing the required entities, attributes, relationships, and constraints.
